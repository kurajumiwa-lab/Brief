# Brief_: a defensible trade loop, not another supply chain operator

**Decision memo · 15 October 2026 · Status: built for a controlled pilot, market fit unproven**

## The decision

Start with **existing vendor-to-vendor relationships in one Kenyan market corridor**. Help a buyer request the *same stock from a known supplier again*, review the proposed price, record the supplier's response and the actual handover, then keep a portable history of what happened. This is a small, auditable workflow, **not** a promise to source inventory, deliver it, finance it or clear payments. Kisumu could be a convenient pilot location, but proximity is not evidence of demand: choose the corridor only after recruiting real repeat-trading pairs.

The contrast with the platforms in the supplied context is useful as a **positioning hypothesis**, not evidence of their current scale, Kenyan coverage, economics or feature set. Do **not** sell Brief_ as a local AppDirect, Tradeshift, Spocket or SAP Business Network, or as another pan-African wholesale marketplace. Their infrastructure and/or aggregation models depend on network density, integrations, capital and operations we do not have. Our potential edge is a consented local relationship plus a small record **both parties can audit**. Whether anyone values that edge enough to change behaviour or pay is still unknown.

**Customer promise to test:** “Reorder with the supplier you already trust. Agree the price, confirm the handover together and keep your own trade history.” Do not say “verified payment,” “receipt,” “tax invoice,” “guaranteed delivery,” “compliant supplier,” or “we find the cheapest stock.”

The existing [Kenya opportunity-network strategy](kenya-opportunity-network-strategy.md) proposes a *different* longer-term hypothesis: verified standing for document-gated suppliers and institutional buyers. Its public-market evidence is **not** interviews, purchase commitments or authority to integrate with a mandatory system. Do not conflate that health/procurement thesis with success of this local trade-record pilot. If that segment later wins, keep its verifier cost, data provenance and buyer adoption gates separate.

## Smallest product and operating boundary

1. **No cold-start marketplace requirement.** Recruit buyer–supplier pairs that already traded at least twice offline; do not promise new suppliers, prices or fulfilment. The buyer explicitly reviews quantity, prior price, supplier's posted price and proposed whole-KES product amount. A retry uses the same idempotency key; it is still a pending request, never a purchase or payment.
2. **No false green tick.** The supplier confirms and dispatches via the existing state machine. The buyer obtains a six-digit code online; only the selected provider enters it. A queued offline acknowledgement is labelled **NOT confirmed** until the server accepts it. `received` means handover acknowledged, while `not_applicable`/`not_started` remains a *separate* settlement state. All records and CSV say they are **not receipts, invoices or tax documents**.
3. **Keep operations with the parties.** The supplier sets their own inventory and pickup details; the selected provider handles handover. Brief_ provides software and narrow technical support, not vans, drivers, warehouses, cash collection, payment dispute adjudication, price-setting or a managed pickup network. No customer/consumer storefront work for this slice.
4. **Kill switches stay off.** `TRADE_PAYMENTS_ENABLED=false`, `TRADE_SETTLEMENT_ENABLED=false`, `TRADE_PLATFORM_FEE_RATE=0.0`, `PSP_PROVIDER=mock`, `TRADE_PSP_SUB_ACCOUNT=""`. No real-money or escrow pilot without separate product, legal, security and provider review. Do not flip a flag to make a demo work.
5. **Not a reputation score.** Display the recorded event and its actor/time; never infer that a supplier is reliable or formally verified from a few movements. Any introduction or external data import needs consent and provenance first.

## Validation, not a launch announcement

**Recruitment (weeks 0–2, proposed):** Select one market corridor; invite ~20 consenting repeat buyer–supplier pairs across a few product lines. Interview both sides about their last two *actual* trades and reconstruct baseline reorder time, missed handovers and record-keeping costs. Exclude invented/sample movements from results. Document who can safely generate/share a receipt code online before an outage. The team is not an intermediary in any trade.

**Observed pilot (weeks 3–6):** Ask pairs to use the new workflow for their next ordinary repeat purchase, *without* requiring a new supplier. Log `order_requested`, `order_repeated`, supplier confirmation, dispatch, `receipt_code_issued`, `received`, cancellations, conflicts and support time. Shadow at least 10 handovers with consent (including a throttled 3G/30-second disconnect); check whether both parties agree with what the ledger says, and record deviations. Compare actual reorder minutes and disputes with each pair's baseline, not with a guessed industry average. Offer a read-only CSV export and ask whether they would keep using it without our assistance.

**90-day decision (proposed target unless already set below):** Count distinct buyer–supplier–stock repeat pairs, **not** clicks or screens. From [`feature-teardown-2026.md` §11](feature-teardown-2026.md), the existing **[Target]** is that at least **30%** of eligible repeat-pair movements start at the reorder surface within 90 days; below that, stop before building pickup points or escrow. Define the denominator as a pair's second or later recorded movement for the same stock line, and the numerator as those with an `order_repeated` event. Report the raw numerator/denominator, by cohort and week; zero eligible repeats is *inconclusive*, not success. Also report:

| Signal | Exact observation | Proposed decision use |
|---|---|---|
| Completion | `received` movements / confirmed movements within seven days; also count still `shipped`, cancelled and expired. | If participants systematically cannot reach a truthful handover without our staff, fix the loop or stop. Do not count offline-queued codes as complete. |
| Retention | Pairs with a second **self-initiated** request 30 days after their first recorded handover / eligible pairs. | Require a credible repeated use pattern before expanding acquisition; do not mistake recruiter-assisted first orders for retention. |
| Accuracy and failure | Mismatched price/quantity, `409` conflicts, rejected codes, acknowledgements still pending after reconnection, disputed outcomes per 100 attempted handovers. | Any uncorrected false “received,” cross-vendor leak or lost pending acknowledgement is a **safety stop**, regardless of conversion. |
| Cost to serve | Support minutes and infrastructure cost per completed trade, separately from supplier/provider fulfilment costs. | No bespoke dispatch, stock buying or manual reconciliation to inflate completion. Do not claim viable unit economics with zero revenue. |
| Willingness to pay | Ask for a priced, cancellable design-partner commitment for *record/reorder software*, not for delivery or credit; count signed commitments and refusals. | Set a price only after repeat use and an explicit paid commitment; feature enthusiasm alone is not a business model. |

**Walk away or split the thesis:** If the reorder gate fails, test a simpler records-only utility or stop the trade wedge. If most value comes from our staff arranging supply or delivery, the hypothesis is an operations business and we should **not** follow it by adding a fleet or pickup agents. Test the document-gated health hypothesis separately: the existing strategy calls for supplier problem ranking, verifier unit cost and buyer shortlist use (see its §1.4) before spending on verification integrations. No tender submission, eTIMS, e-GP or KEMSA integration is implied by this pilot.

## Engineering evidence, limitations and release gate

- API: `GET /api/orders/repeatable`, `POST /api/orders/repeat` (`Idempotency-Key`), `GET /api/orders/records`, `GET /api/orders/records.csv` share the existing authenticated order router. Repeat stock availability is locked and rechecked. A unique buyer/key index and advisory lock guard double-taps; the reused key with different details is a 409. CSV pages are capped, vendor-scoped and formula-escaped; historical prices come from snapshots. Migration: `backend/alembic/versions/0020_reorder_key.py`.
- UI: `/orders/repeat`, `/orders/records`, `/orders/:id` receipt card and the home trade overview. The first-run overview has no fake zero stat. Search/export are restricted to a bounded record page. The receipt UI distinguishes local pending, server-confirmed, expired and rejected states; it clears a queued code on logout and scopes it to vendor + order within the browser tab.
- Automated checks at the time of this memo: the full backend suite passed **229** tests; the full frontend suite passed **173** tests. Frontend lint has **0 errors, 10 pre-existing warnings**; the production build succeeds. Automated tests do **not** substitute for observing a real handover under network throttling.
- **Do not claim fully offline handover readiness yet.** A code already issued by the server remains readable/shareable on the buyer's open screen during an outage, and a provider-entered code can queue for automatic reconciliation on reconnection/reopen in the same browser session. But the buyer **cannot create or invalidate a code offline** without server authority; the queue does not survive the browser session being cleared, and a code may expire before connectivity returns. That conflicts with the directive's offline-regeneration/30-second field acceptance unless the real pilot's handovers can reliably pre-issue codes online. Require a security-reviewed protocol (not a client-generated fake receipt) or constrain the pilot and explicitly mark P0 offline acceptance **not met**.
- **Export timing is explicit:** the UI creates CSV from the **displayed, server-capped page**, so it exports exactly the rows and goods/settlement states the vendor saw and can do so during a disconnection. The direct authenticated `/records.csv` API is separately paginated/capped and re-queries at request time; a new trade between two separate API calls can change its result. Neither export is an immutable bank statement or proof of a later state.

**Go/no-go:** permit only a consented, small, no-money software pilot when the end-to-end throttled-connection exercise and privacy review pass. Broader P0 rollout waits for a safe offline-code plan if the directive's offline-regeneration acceptance remains mandatory. Any metrics or competitor claims not established in this repo or the pilot remain hypotheses, not marketing copy.
